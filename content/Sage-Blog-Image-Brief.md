# Sage Psychological Services — Blog Image Brief

Companion to the main site image brief. Same rules: warm and forward-looking tone, no identifiable AI-generated faces, every prompt complete and ready to paste.

11 articles · 22 image slots · 17 AI-generated · 5 to be photographed for real · Prepared 24 July 2026

---

## What the blog currently does

Every article uses the same two-slot template: a hero image below the title, and a CTA banner near the end. All eleven CTA banners are currently the same file — `wa-sunrise.jpg` — which means a reader moving through two or three articles sees the identical image every time. That repetition is the most visible problem in the set and the easiest to fix.

The heroes are more varied but still drawn from the same generic nature pool as the service pages, so a CBT explainer and a Medicare admin guide are illustrated with interchangeable landscapes. Nothing in the current set tells the reader what kind of article they have landed on.

### How these prompts differ from the service pages

Blog readers arrive from search, usually earlier in their thinking than someone browsing the service pages, and they are reading to *understand* rather than to *choose*. So the imagery here leans further toward the concrete and the practical — worksheets, diaries, paperwork, equipment, an ordinary front door — and further away from atmosphere. Each hero is chosen to signal the article's genre within a second of landing: an explainer looks like an explainer, an admin guide looks administrative, a local guide looks local.

Each article below opens with a short read on what the piece is actually trying to do, because that is what determined the image choice. Where an article carries a crisis-support block or an explicit non-diagnostic framing, the imagery is deliberately restrained — those are noted individually.

### Why 5 slots are marked for real photography

Five articles are doing work that a generated image actively undermines. The EMDR explainer opens by admitting the therapy sounds implausible, and a photograph of the real device is the fastest way to defeat that. The first-session article describes the actual room in words. The Fremantle guide is a direct comparison against other local practitioners and names the real address. And the trauma-signs and EMDR-vs-talk articles both close on the room itself. These overlap almost entirely with the interiors already scheduled in the main brief, so the same half-day at Silas Street covers them — just shoot extra angles.

## Reminders

Paste each prompt whole — realism, face-safety, skin and negative instructions are already merged in. Don't add camera models or words like "hyper-realistic" or "cinematic".

Generate four or five per prompt and keep the worst-composed result. Then degrade before publishing: 2–4% monochrome noise, saturation down 8–12%, blacks lifted slightly, very light unsharp mask, export JPEG at quality 82–88.

Two prompts carry hard content constraints worth double-checking in the output. **B3** (therapy vs medication) must contain no pills, packaging or medication props of any kind, since the article is careful to stay neutral on a decision that belongs with a GP. **B10** (Medicare) must contain no readable text, no forms, no cards and no government branding.
